We can build the important technology ourselves—starting at launch.

The first decision is where licensed listing data comes from. We can pay for inexpensive IDX display and build our own CRM/nurturing immediately, or—if MLS PIN authorizes it—build the entire property search, CRM and nurturing system as one custom WordPress platform. RealScout remains a feature-rich benchmark, not our only launch option.

IDX Broker handles the approved MLS display. We build the WordPress CRM, agent portal, smart workflows and nurturing. Baseline: $80 for two agents plus the currently listed $7/month MLS PIN fee.

03 / The visibility advantage — explained simply

Why our own website matters: being found before someone calls an agent.

A plain-English guide for the brokerage team. Our CRM and nurturing system helps us stay in touch with buyers and sellers we already know. Our own well-built website helps new people discover us, trust us, and become leads in the first place. Both parts matter.

“
Think of it like owning our storefront on the busiest streets in town.

A beautiful office helps when someone walks in. SEO and AI discoverability help people find the office before they even know its name. A custom website lets us build that visibility on a domain we control, instead of relying entirely on someone else's platform.

Being useful when someone asks a question. We publish clear, accurate answers about buying, selling, neighborhoods, and the process—written or reviewed by real agents.

The distinction: Automated nurturing can help turn a lead into an engaged client. A search-friendly website helps create opportunities to earn that lead in the first place. A custom IDX and nurture platform could eventually connect both jobs inside one system.

What we control by owning the WordPress website

Our website becomes a long-term business asset

  • Our domain and content. Local guides, agent pages, blog posts, and useful resources live at an address we control—even if our IDX or nurturing provider changes.
  • Our own leads and measurement. Brokerage-approved forms, analytics, conversion tracking, and CRM routing make it easier to understand which content produces real inquiries.
  • Our brand and expertise. We can feature the office, its agents, neighborhood knowledge, testimonials with permission, and practical seller/buyer education.
  • Our future flexibility. Switching vendors doesn't require abandoning all of our website copy, links, content history or organic discovery work.

Why simply embedding listings isn't enough

  • Every brokerage may show the same homes. The original value comes from what our agents know about the market—not from copying the same listing descriptions.
  • Search and AI engines need accessible information. Fast mobile pages, meaningful headings, internal links and crawlable text help them understand our content.
  • Listing-page SEO has limits. IDX data use, indexing, attribution and listing-display rights must follow MLS rules; duplicate listing feeds alone are not a reliable ranking strategy.
  • No vendor guarantees ranking. A third-party service can coexist with excellent SEO, but controlling the main WordPress site gives us more flexibility to build, test and improve it.

What we should actually publish and build

Practical first 90 days

  4. Helpful FAQs and explainers: financing steps, offer process, contingencies and what happens at closing, kept current and reviewed by agents.
  1. Local proof and updates: broker-reviewed market commentary, relevant case studies, client stories with consent and updates tied to actual local knowledge.
  2. Technical foundation: clean URLs, good mobile speed, sitemap, logical headings, internal links, structured business information and proper canonical handling.
  3. Local presence: keep the eligible office's Google Business Profile and contact details accurate and consistent; earn authentic reviews.
  4. Lead tracking: use Search Console, analytics and lead-source fields to measure which visits become conversations and appointments.
